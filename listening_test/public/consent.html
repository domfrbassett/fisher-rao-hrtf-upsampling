You are invited to take part in a headphone listening study about spatial hearing. Before deciding whether to take part, please read this page carefully. You may ask the researcher any questions before beginning.

Purpose of the Study

This study investigates how accurately people can hear small changes in the direction of a sound over headphones. The sounds are created using recordings that describe how a listener's head, torso and ears filter sound from different directions. These recordings are called head-related transfer functions, or HRTFs.

The research compares original measured spatial-audio recordings with reconstructed versions made from fewer measured directions. Your responses will help assess whether the reconstructed versions preserve the small directional differences that listeners can hear.

What You Will Do

You will hear pairs of brief noise sounds over headphones. On each trial, sound A and sound B will be played. Your task is to choose which sound was farther to the left or higher up, depending on the instruction for that section.

The study begins with a headphone side and listening-level check. You should hear one sound on the left followed by one sound on the right. The task then asks you to make left-right or higher-up judgements. Each section starts with sounds that are easier to tell apart and then changes the difference between them based on your responses. Trials do not provide feedback or replay. You may take short breaks when prompted between sections.

The study is split into left-right and vertical sections. Each section is expected to take approximately 15-20 minutes, depending on response time. You may complete later sections by returning with the same participant code.

Who Should Take Part

You should take part only if you are able to listen comfortably over headphones in a quiet environment. Please do not take part if you have current ear pain, a recent ear infection, or any condition that makes headphone listening uncomfortable.

Risks and Discomforts

The sounds are brief and should be played at a comfortable level. The main possible discomforts are listening fatigue, difficulty judging the spatial position, or annoyance from repeated noise sounds. Set the volume during the audio check and do not increase it during the task. You may stop at any time if the sounds become uncomfortable.

This task is not a hearing test and does not provide clinical information about your hearing.

Voluntary Participation and Withdrawal

Taking part is voluntary. You may stop the study at any time without giving a reason. If you withdraw before completing the task, close the browser or tell the researcher. If you want your saved responses removed after taking part, contact the researcher and provide your participant code, unless the data have already been fully anonymised or aggregated.

Ethics and Participant Concerns

If you have concerns about the conduct of the study that you do not wish to raise directly with the researcher, you may contact the BCU ethics team at BCU_Ethics@bcu.ac.uk.

Data Collected

The study records your participant code, listening environment category, session identifier, confirmation that you consented and used headphones, trial responses, whether each response matched the programmed answer, reaction times, playback counts, trial condition labels, and the sound-direction information associated with each trial. The study server also stores browser user-agent information for technical troubleshooting.

The study does not intentionally record your name, speech, webcam video, microphone audio, or screen contents. Do not enter identifying personal information as your participant code.

Confidentiality and Data Use

Responses are stored under a participant code rather than a name. Data will be analysed to evaluate how accurately listeners judge small changes in sound direction across measured and reconstructed spatial-audio conditions. Results may be reported in a dissertation, presentation, publication, or related research output in aggregated form.

Raw response files will be accessible only to the researcher and authorised supervisors or assessors. The data will be stored, archived and deleted in line with Birmingham City University research data-management requirements.

Birmingham City University's research participant privacy notice explains how personal information is used in research, including data protection rights and safeguards.
